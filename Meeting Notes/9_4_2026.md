## Questions for Stakeholders
“Our baby gets too close to the stairs, so I want to be notified of it without scaring the baby.”


Opening:
Intros, thank them
State purpose: "We're here to learn about your situation, not pitch anything. So don’t worry, there are no wrong answers here."
Ask permission to record
"We'll start with some background, then get into stories."

Questions (ORDERED):
BACKGROUND:

- Zach: How old is the baby, and how are they getting around right now? (crawling, walking, climbing)?
Important question for speed and reach
The baby being a crawler vs. a walker possible changes the height of sensors and warning distance

- Aedan: Who else is in the house, and would they be walking near the stairs too? (pets included)
Important for finding out about false triggers
Listen for pets, siblings, or adults passing the stairs often

- Lucas: What’s the layout of the stairs/room in question? (maybe ask for a photo or a sketch)
Ask so we can know about sensor placement and range
Note distances, doorways, and if it is an open or enclosed stairs

STORIES:

- Lucas: Walk me through a normal day at home. So, where are you, and where’s the baby?
Listen for when the baby is being unwatched

- Zach: Could you tell us what exactly happened the last time the baby got near the stairs?
Listen for any real incident, specifically how they noticed, how fast they reacted, and how the baby got there 

- AEDAN: What have you tried and why hasn’t it worked? 
Want to learn about what failed and why so we don’t repeat it
Look for specific complaints (too loud, baby climbed it, etc.) got it 

- Lucas: When are you most likely to lose track of where the baby is? 
Important for finding out about high risk moments
Listen for things such as cooking or showering, that is when our device must work
- Zach: Where are you usually in the house when the baby is moving around?
Ask so we know how far the alert needs to reach (same room vs. other floor means different design)

DEEPER:

- Lucas: Has anything scared the baby before? Quick follow up: What did it look like?
Listen for what scary actually means to them (noise, light, etc.)

- Aedan: This might be a bit specific, but has the baby ever gotten used to or started ignoring something (like gates, noises, or toys for example)? 
Find out whether a deterrent would stop working 
Listen for stories of the baby ignoring things

SPECS:

- Aedan: How would you like to find out when the baby is near the stairs? And, what do you consider to be annoying or disruptive?
Find out what alert type they want (sound, phone, light)
Listen for what they hate

- Zach: Once you are alerted, how long does it usually take you to reach the baby?
Find out how early we must detect the baby (perhaps listen for a number of seconds)

- Aedan: How do you plan to use it? 
We want to know if it’ll always be on, or if there is an on/off toggle, or if it needs to be moved around
- Zach: Does it matter what it looks like or where it goes in the room?
Gives us constraints on size, mounting, and wires (NO WIRES COMING OUT OF IT)
If they say “no”, it gives us freedom

- Lucas: What would you define as a successful product?
Ask this so we can find out what their definition of “done” is
Listen for some sort of measurable statement that we can turn into a requirement

CLOSE:

- Aedan: Is there anything else we should know? SOMEBODY HERRO THIS QUESTION REAL QUICK
Probably going to be the best question in this entire interview
