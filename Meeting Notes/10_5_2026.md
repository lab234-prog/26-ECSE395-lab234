# Design Team Meeting Notes

- **Sensors:** Use the **motion sensor** for wide-area detection (it covers a larger space and only needs to detect motion) and the **ultrasonic sensor for "above" / adult sensing** (smaller range, focuses on what is higher up). This responds to feedback that the design should also detect adults.
- **Vibration sensor:** Add it to the diagram. It is simple, with 3 pins (power, ground, data out).
- **Motion sensor wiring:** Has less wiring than the ultrasonic sensor. The ultrasonic sensor is expected to be the hardest part.
- **Mounting:** Attach the box **to the gate**, using the stakeholder-preferred design with the back plate (second idea, week 5 stakeholder slides in Google Drive).
- **Ultrasonic orientation (MVP):** Fixed, pointing **upright or diagonal**. A ratcheting pivot for adjustable angle would be ideal but is difficult to CAD; defer it.
- **Box shape idea (Zach):** Box with one side cut at an angle (filleted) to aim the sensor upward without extra mechanisms.
- **Bluetooth module:** Remove it from the design.
- **Stakeholder tolerance:** Stakeholders said they are fine with **false negatives**, so the system shouldn't be overly annoying.
- Idea floated: alert gets louder as someone gets closer (smaller range for the ultrasonic).
- Motion sensor suits the layout: the area near the stairs is small, so anything entering it would be detected.
- Lucas keeps the status lights in the design.
- Lucas has built a power and data diagram; the component list will be posted in the meeting notes.

### Content ideas for E (unknowns, assumptions, risks)
- Specs of the components are not fully known.
- Currently assuming ideal operating conditions.
- Risks: human error (improper mounting, low power), sensor accuracy, human usability.
- Environmental robustness: the team lacks full knowledge of the home and gate setup.

### Content ideas for F (prototype strategy)
1. **Cardboard mock-up** of the box to check component fit, layout, and fire hazards.
2. Fit components inside and make them function; test sensors.
3. **3D print** the box and verify everything works.
- Prototype the box first because it is easier.
- Sensor testing can include role-playing at different height levels (standing, sitting, crawling) to check high and low sensing. This also feeds next week's deliverable on **testing specs**.

### Content ideas for G (learning and iteration plan)
- Evidence for design decisions: sensor test results and box fit.
- Learning from failure: if something doesn't work, the team learns more (e.g. box too big/small, sensors not working as expected).
- Prototype findings guide design changes; features may be cut to keep the product usable.
